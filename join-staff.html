Volunteering

Volunteering Program

We warmly welcome volunteers of all backgrounds throughout the year. We believe everyone has something unique to offer, and encourage exchange and collaboration — the more diverse the group, the greater the opportunity for new perspectives.

Ask about volunteering
"Love is not patronizing and charity isn't about pity, it is about love. Charity and love are the same — with charity you give love, so don't just give money but reach out your hand instead."— Mother Teresa

Types of volunteering

One-day sharing

A single day contributing a skill or activity to the school community.

One-week project

A focused week-long contribution, any time of year.

German week

Last week of June — language and cultural exchange.

Peace & Community Week

A themed week around community-building and non-violent communication.

Community Committees

Family participation is at the heart of The Chestnut Tree

Families contribute not only as parents, but as active members of a shared educational community — bringing their time, skills, culture, and care to sustain something that benefits every child. Four committees welcome hands-on involvement:

Celebrations & Welcome

Welcoming new families and supporting community gatherings.

Infrastructure

Taking care of shared spaces and supporting maintenance needs.

Kitchen

Promoting a healthy, local, and sustainable food culture.

Excursions

Supporting approved excursions and practical logistics.

What volunteering builds on

  • Grounding in our 4 Pillars: Care for Oneself, Others, Nature, and the Future of Our Planet
  • Individualized curriculums for the children volunteers work with
  • Community engagement and non-violent communication, including monthly parent classes on emotional intelligence and cooperation

The Chestnut Tree reserves the right to decline a volunteer application if a profile does not align with the community's vision or our ability to provide proper resources for development.

A volunteer story

Building a greenhouse, together

Thomas Betschart, a passionate nature enthusiast, shared his expertise in permaculture and mineralogy with our students and actively engaged them in building a greenhouse — hands-on, start to finish. Visits like his continue to benefit our students for years to come.
